# Product

## Product Status

This project is a **high-fidelity prototype/demo website** for **Laxmikrupa Emporium / Laxmi Krupa | Men's Wear**.

The prototype is being created using currently available public information, shop imagery, social media content, and other currently available business information.

This is **not the final production specification**.

The complete product catalog, pricing, inventory, business policies, payment methods, shipping rules, customer accounts, ordering workflows, operational processes, and other business requirements will be confirmed directly with the business owner if the project proceeds to production.

The prototype must therefore be designed and architected so that provisional/demo content can later be replaced with confirmed business data without requiring the frontend experience to be rebuilt from scratch.

---

# 1. Brand Identity

**Business Name:** Laxmikrupa Emporium

**Brand Reference:** Laxmi Krupa | Men's Wear

**Location:** Surat, Gujarat, India

**Business Type:** Men's apparel retail with festive, event, family/group, and bulk-order offerings based on currently available information.

The website must use the real business identity.

Do not create a fictional fashion-house identity, fictional atelier name, fictional heritage story, or fictional luxury brand positioning.

---

# 2. Prototype Purpose

The purpose of this project is to create a visually impressive and realistic demonstration of how Laxmikrupa Emporium could present itself digitally.

The prototype should be strong enough to:

- demonstrate the quality of the proposed website
- communicate the visual direction to the business owner
- showcase the shop's currently known product categories
- demonstrate product discovery
- demonstrate product presentation
- demonstrate festive and group-order experiences
- demonstrate direct inquiry / WhatsApp interactions
- establish a premium and memorable digital identity
- provide a strong foundation for the eventual production website

The prototype is **not required to implement the complete production commerce system**.

Production functionality will be added or modified after the business requirements are confirmed.

---

# 3. Prototype Data Principle

Information currently available about the business is incomplete.

Therefore:

- Use verified/currently available information wherever possible.
- Use realistic demo data where actual information is unavailable.
- Keep demo data clearly structured and easy to replace.
- Do not present invented information as confirmed business facts.
- Do not invent brand history, manufacturing heritage, certifications, suppliers, fabric origins, or other claims.
- Do not invent unsupported product categories.
- Do not assume final pricing.
- Do not assume final inventory.
- Do not assume final shipping policies.
- Do not assume final payment methods.
- Do not assume final return/refund policies.

When real business information becomes available, the corresponding demo content should be replaceable without requiring major UI restructuring.

# 8. Design Principles

## 8.1 Editorial Immersion

Products should be presented like fashion editorial content rather than ordinary ecommerce tiles.

Use:

- strong photography
- asymmetric compositions
- intentional cropping
- varied visual scale
- editorial typography
- deliberate whitespace
- carefully controlled visual rhythm

Avoid making every section look like a conventional ecommerce grid.

---

## 8.2 Garments Remain the Hero

The clothing must remain the primary visual focus.

Typography, animation, backgrounds, effects, and decorative elements should support the garments rather than compete with them.

Color should primarily come from:

- garments
- festive collections
- campaign photography
- subtle architectural surfaces

---

## 8.3 Clarity Over Decoration

The website should feel sophisticated without becoming visually noisy.

Avoid:

- excessive gradients
- excessive glassmorphism
- unnecessary glowing elements
- excessive rounded cards
- generic SaaS layouts
- excessive floating UI
- animation for the sake of animation
- excessive shadows
- unnecessary decorative icons

Every visual element should have a reason to exist.

---

## 8.4 Tactile Interaction

Interactions should feel responsive, weighted, and intentional.

Examples:

- image hover transitions
- subtle product image movement
- magnetic or tactile CTAs where appropriate
- smooth drawer transitions
- size selector interactions
- filter transitions
- editorial reveal animations
- subtle scroll choreography

Motion should enhance the experience rather than distract from the clothing.

# 19. Performance Principles

The prototype should remain performant despite its visual ambition.

Priorities:

- avoid unnecessary dependencies
- avoid unnecessarily large bundles
- lazy-load heavy assets
- preserve image aspect ratios
- prevent layout shifts
- optimize large images
- avoid unnecessary JavaScript
- prefer CSS when CSS is sufficient
- code-split heavy interactive experiences when appropriate

Advanced visual technologies such as:

- WebGL
- shaders
- canvas
- particles
- 3D
- Spline
- complex animation engines

are permitted only when they provide meaningful visual value.

They should never be added simply because they are available.

---

# 20. Advanced Visual Technology Evaluation

When considering advanced visual effects, evaluate:

1. Does it meaningfully improve the visual/editorial experience?
2. Does it maintain smooth interaction and strong frame rate?
3. Does it behave appropriately on mobile?
4. Does it have a reduced-motion fallback?
5. Can it be lazy-loaded or code-split?
6. Is the implementation maintainable?
7. Does it improve the product rather than merely demonstrate technology?

If the answer is no, prefer:

- modern CSS
- optimized imagery
- lightweight JavaScript
- simple transforms
- opacity transitions

---

# 21. Component & Library Philosophy

External component libraries and design resources are **optional resources**, not mandatory dependencies.

The implementation may discover and evaluate appropriate components from sources such as:

- React Bits
- 21st.dev
- Magic UI
- Unlumen UI
- Smooth UI
- Animaster
- Skiper UI
- UIverse
- Forge UI
- OriginKit
- other appropriate UI/component sources

Components should be selected based on whether they genuinely improve the experience.

Do not use a component merely because it exists in a library.

When an external component is selected:

- inspect its implementation
- adapt it to the established design system
- preserve the project's architectural consistency
- avoid unnecessary dependencies
- avoid duplicating functionality
- prefer extracting only the useful implementation when practical

---

# 22. Animation Philosophy

Animation should communicate:

- hierarchy
- continuity
- depth
- interaction
- state changes
- editorial storytelling

Use:

- CSS transitions for simple interactions
- GSAP when complex choreography is genuinely justified
- Lenis when smooth scrolling materially improves the experience
- lightweight native browser APIs when sufficient

Avoid stacking multiple attention-grabbing effects.

The website should feel **smooth and expensive**, not overloaded.

- keyboard navigation

- appropriate ARIA attributes
- accessible dialogs/drawers
- accessible buttons
- accessible form controls
- sufficient text contrast
- sensible touch targets
- reduced-motion support

Visual sophistication must not come at the expense of usability.

# 26. Data Architecture Principle

Business data should be separated from presentation wherever practical.

For example:

```text
src/
├── data/
│   ├── products/
│   ├── categories/
│   └── collections/
│
├── components/
├── pages/
├── layouts/
├── services/
├── hooks/
├── styles/
└── assets/
```

The prototype should make it straightforward to replace demo product data with real backend/API data later.

Do not hardcode business information throughout unrelated UI components when a structured data model can be used instead.

# 28. Success Criteria

The prototype succeeds when:

1. It clearly represents Laxmikrupa Emporium rather than a fictional fashion brand.
2. The currently known product categories are represented accurately.
3. Unsupported products are not presented as part of the store.
4. The experience feels premium and professionally designed.
5. The website does not look like a generic ecommerce template.
6. Product photography and garments remain visually dominant.
7. Navigation and product discovery are intuitive.
8. Interactions feel tactile and purposeful.
9. The experience works well across desktop and mobile.
10. Performance remains strong despite the visual design.
11. Demo data can later be replaced with real business data.
12. The architecture provides a practical foundation for the future production website.

---

# 29. Final Principle

Build the prototype as if it could become the real website, but do not pretend that the prototype already contains the client's final requirements.

The visual experience should be ambitious.

The business claims should be conservative.

The architecture should be flexible.

The data should be replaceable.

The design should feel custom-made for Laxmikrupa Emporium rather than generated from a generic ecommerce template.

The current goal is to create a compelling prototype that can be presented to the business owner and used as the foundation for the final production website after the project is approved.
